About This Dashboard

1. Average Investor Equity Allocation

From Jesse Livermore's Philosophical Economics: the percentage of financial assets that U.S. households allocate to equities is the single best predictor of subsequent 10-year real returns. The metric comes from the Federal Reserve's Z.1 Flow of Funds report.

Formula: Equity Allocation = Household Equity Holdings / (Equity Holdings + Debt Securities + Deposits + Other Financial Assets)

FRED Series: BOGZ1FL153064486Q (Directly and Indirectly Held Corporate Equities as a Percentage of Financial Assets)

2. Shiller CAPE Ratio

Robert Shiller's Cyclically Adjusted Price-to-Earnings ratio smooths earnings over 10 years to remove business cycle effects. It has historically mean-reverted around ~16-17.

Formula: CAPE = Current Real Price / Average Real Earnings over prior 10 years

3. P/IE — Price to Inflation-Adjusted Earnings

From O'Shaughnessy Asset Management's "The Earnings Mirage": standard reported earnings overstate true economic earnings during inflationary periods because of FIFO inventory accounting (understates cost of goods sold) and historical-cost depreciation (understates replacement cost). P/IE corrects for these effects.

Adjustment: Real Earnings = Reported Earnings − Inventory Inflation Gain − Depreciation Shortfall

4. Unemployment Rate & Moving Average

The unemployment rate relative to its 12-month moving average serves as a recession indicator. When unemployment crosses above its trend, it signals economic deterioration. Related to the Sahm Rule.

5. S&P 500 & 200-Day Moving Average

The most widely followed trend indicator. When the S&P 500 trades above its 200-day moving average, the trend is up. Below signals a potential bear market. Simple but historically effective at reducing drawdowns.

6. CPI & Inflation

The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) tracks the cost of a basket of goods. Year-over-year changes measure the inflation rate, which affects both valuations and monetary policy.

7. VIX & Volatility Regimes

The CBOE Volatility Index measures implied volatility from S&P 500 options. Low-volatility regimes (VIX < 20) tend to favor trend-following strategies. High-volatility spikes (VIX > 30) often coincide with market bottoms but also precede further declines.

8. Treasury Yield Curve Spread (10Y - 2Y)

The yield curve spread measures the difference between long-term (10-year) and short-term (2-year) Treasury yields. Under normal conditions, long-term rates are higher than short-term rates, reflecting the term premium for lending money further into the future.

When the curve inverts (spread goes negative), it signals that bond markets expect future rate cuts — typically because they anticipate an economic downturn. An inverted yield curve has preceded every U.S. recession since 1970, though with variable lead times (6-24 months).

FRED Series: T10Y2Y (10-Year Treasury Constant Maturity Minus 2-Year Treasury Constant Maturity)

Nowcasting & Publication Lags

FRED data has significant publication lags. To provide the most current signal, this dashboard nowcasts lagged series:

Equity Allocation: FRED's Z.1 data lags ~3 months. We estimate current allocation by applying S&P 500 price changes to the last known reading: if equities were X% of the portfolio and the S&P moved by Y%, the new equity share is recomputed assuming non-equity assets are unchanged.

Unemployment: Released ~1 month after reference. We use weekly Initial Jobless Claims (ICSA) to estimate the direction and magnitude of the next reading.

CPI: Released ~2 months after reference. We extrapolate using the 3-month average month-over-month trend.

Nowcast points appear as dashed pink lines on charts and are labeled in the strategy signals.

Lag-Aware Backtesting

The backtest accounts for realistic publication delays. At each monthly decision point, the strategy only uses data that would have actually been available:

S&P 500 / VIX / Yield Curve: Real-time (0 months lag) · Unemployment: 1 month lag · CPI / CAPE: 2 months lag · Equity Allocation: 1 month lag (since we're nowcasting)

This eliminates look-ahead bias and gives a more honest picture of what an investor could have achieved.
